## My Contribution

Collaborating on the game *Undercooked* over the past few weeks with my teammates Jordan Swallow, Noralee Yarra, Dorlen Gomez and Nancy Peters was a humbling experience. My contributions within our game centered on designing the introductory page, the narrative, and the audio elements for our creation. As these are the first elements a player encounters, it led me to reflect on the impact initial impressions create even before they engage with the mathematical or culinary game mechanics.

Fullerton (2019) had noted that a strong game concept begins with a compelling premise that grounds players in the logic and context of play. <Cite n={1} title="Fullerton (2019)" /> By establishing a simple, cross-cultural narrative that contextualized the game as a chef working in a global foods festival, we created a naturally unfolding situation where cultural and mathematical opportunities collide through unique culinary requests. This connects to my previous Intellectual Productions on representation and how inclusion can either deepen or undercut educational potential depending upon the game's core systems. By featuring global cuisines and centering student identity amongst the challenges of measuring and assembling ingredients, we followed through with meaningful design choices that enhanced the game's overall scope and systems of play.


## Technical Challenges

Utilizing Genially for this project presented some trade-offs worth bringing up. While the learning curve of the graphical interface was initially a deterrent for me, the platform facilitated seamless real-time collaboration among my team with varying technical backgrounds. While Genially's technical limitations grew into a barrier in manifesting what we had envisioned, we were able to provide enough of a foundation to show how our game could be implemented if we had chosen an alternative tech-stack. Fullerton (2019) also described meaningful play as a condition where player choices lead to outcomes that are both discernible and integrated into the larger system. <Cite n={1} title="Fullerton (2019)" /> Unable to fully demonstrate the scoring and variable management we had envisioned, we could only gesture towards what that would look like within our game rather than physically encode it.


## Designing for Meaningful Outcomes

Reflecting on my field notes of the game *DrainSim*, *Undercooked* provided a sharper lens for analyzing our work. Gee (2008) describes games as environments that foreground specific aspects of a problem while backgrounding others to keep play manageable. <Cite n={2} title="Gee (2008)" /> *DrainSim*'s early levels were so heavily guided that I found myself simply following instructions rather than developing my own internal logic. I initially worried that *Undercooked* risked falling into the same trap: without true variable-based systems, student choices remain merely cosmetic rather than systemically impactful. With more development time, rebuilding the prototype in a browser-native framework could enable dynamic, individualized logic. This would ensure outcomes are genuinely tied to students' reasoning, rather than simply rewarding procedural compliance.

Gee and Gee (2017) describe learning as a process of conversations with the world, testing, revising, and building understanding across tools and contexts. <Cite n={3} title="Gee & Gee (2017)" />
*Undercooked* reached for this dialogue. While the premise provided the right foundation, a foundation only matters if the system allows for the complexity of building on top of it. That is the work I would go back and finish.
