## Reflective Statement

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

Looking back at my participation throughout ETEC 522, I can see how my contributions near the start shaped my approach to the venture pitch concept I presented at the end. The early weeks brought forth a distinct changemaker mindset: I described myself as an intrapreneur who "picks and chooses which tide to ride and which to swim against," framed investing as building "a toolbox of well-worn, reliable tools," and named my ride as one that remixes what exists, pointing out that "you do not always need to create original ideas to create original value."

This led to co-developing the Adaptive Technology and IEPs OER and considering edutech markets as a whole. I had the chance to review twelve of peers' ventures to which I had shared my insights towards venture's like CISP Esports' ability to capture a student's "competitive drive intrinsically" and Convia's capacity for "shifting technology from an individual activity to a shared physical space," as well as other intelligent ventures that sharpened my EVA approach. These collaborative threads and interactions throughout the course eventually led a venture pitch of my own where I was able to capitalize on my own skillsets and knowledge to share something I saw potential in.

Across the cohort, my contributions were fairly consistent across each of the Canvas Discussions, the OER modules, the Knowledge Mill Blog, and within my own posts in a way that was genuine and collaborative as a peer-EVA towards my colleagues in my reviews, comments and the facilitation of our OER.

Ultimately, what I bring to the future of learning is the ability to "remix" the world around me and "jazz things up" by knowing how the game works and how the pieces fit together. As my final contribution highlighted, "I believe the future of learning is temporarily owned by those who are able to identify and navigate that very space of institutional and marketplace frictions." That is the contribution I hope to keep making: carving out a corner of the market as an independent social enterprise rather than taking on the whole of it.

While I dropped the ball and submitted my final venture pitch a day late, I am grateful to have had the opportunity to have been able to attempt at meaningfully creating and pitching something I saw value in. While this resulted in no peers of mine be able to review what I had pitched, this in itself was a lesson in passion, realistic project scope, and my own abilities as I watched my EVA pitch opportunity set sail before I was ready to share what I had.

Ultimately, across the thirteen weeks, I contributed over two dozen comments to the course blog, a handful of my own posts, I critically engaged and developed my skills as an EVA and genuinely tested my technological abilities in created a realistic mockup of the venture pitch I had dreamt of. That said, I would objectively place myself in the top 20–25% of the cohort.
