## Reflective Statement

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

I entered ETEC 522 as an Educator-Developer-Musician, aiming to bridge creative educational composition with the risk-aware discipline of an Educational Venture Analyst — and to move from commenting to building. The ten postings above chart that arc.

The early weeks fixed my changemaker mindset: I described myself as someone who "picks and chooses which tide to ride and which to swim against," and framed investing as deliberately building "a toolbox of well-worn, reliable tools." That instinct hardened into analysis. Co-developing the [Adaptive Technology & IEPs OER](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-20-adaptive-technology-ieps-oer/) taught me to design for student agency and data sovereignty, while my reviews of [Freedrum](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2025/07/18/freedrum-3-0-the-future-of-digital-drumming/#comment-6155) — "I am buying the venture, not the dream" — and [Pool Maro's](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2024/01/28/pitch-pool-maro/#comment-6156) eleven-year diagnosis gap trained me to spot where a pitch's story and its economics diverge. My [Opportunity Audit](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-28-etec522-analyst-report/) of Sight Reading Factory asked whether useful tools always make sound investments, and my comments on [microlearning](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/06/28/an-opportunity-forecast-usable-microlearning/#comment-6268) ("a Timbit of microknowledge") and the critical-thinking team's "[Calibrated Doubt](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/07/26/week-11-artificial-intelligence-critical-thinking/#comment-6433)" kept the focus on whether the thinking still lands with the learner.

Across the cohort I kept threads collaborative rather than competitive — praising the immersive team's tiered implementation model, probing [CISP Esports'](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/08/12/cisp-esports-academy/#comment-6756) hardware costs, and ending comments with open-ended prompts that handed the conversation back. Where analysis stopped, I built: the [venture pitch](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-08-09-curator-introduction/) for Curator and its Cadenza Studio MVP turned theory into a working prototype.

When considering who owns the future of learning, my thoughts return to the inherent dysfunction of the education marketplace. Throughout this course, I observed how money and decision-making consistently pool far away from the frontlines of the classroom — an entanglement of governments, individuals, and corporations all carving out ownership of this space while remaining disconnected from its actual end-users. As it stands, the future of learning is temporarily owned by those who can identify and navigate that space of institutional and marketplace friction.

Margaret Mead suggests that a small group of thoughtful, committed citizens can change the world. Earlier in my career, I would have swum against every systemic tide to be that change, while playing it safe within my own perspectives of the industry. Evaluating venture pitches and exploring emerging technology markets revealed how much a careful alignment of forces and research can accomplish; one does not always need to take on the whole market to champion something meaningful — sometimes operating within the cracks is enough.

I believe I have the capacity to be that impact. What I bring to the future of learning is the ability to "remix" the world around me, "jazz things up," and take on the future by knowing how the game works and how the pieces fit together. The future of learning will not be owned by a single centralized entity; it is ours to champion, and Margaret Mead's quote captures what a calculated, compounding effort can make possible in the hands of the right changemaker. Measured against the cohort's weblog activity, I place my contribution in the top 10%.

</div>

## Constructive Suggestions

Two suggestions for future iterations of ETEC 522, offered with gratitude for a course that rewarded showing up:

- **Reduce platform friction.** The WordPress comment system silently rejected posts containing hyperlinks or emoji (a database error), costing participants hours and cluttering threads with test replies. A clearer error message, or a markdown-friendly comment field, would protect the very conversational flow this portfolio is meant to reward.
- **Pilot "VC Partner" Peer Circles.** Pair each learner with a small, stable circle of investors who review one another's pitches across the term, so critical feedback is sustained and structured rather than concentrated in the final weeks.
