## Reflective Statement

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

Looking back at my participation throughout ETEC 522, it is interesting to see how my engagements came to shape the final venture pitch concept I shared.

The early weeks brought forth a changemaker mindset: I described myself as someone who "picks and chooses which tide to ride and which to swim against," framed investing as building "a toolbox of well-worn, reliable tools," and named my ride as one that remixes what exists rather than composing from scratch.

Inspired by the OER discussions, that instinct sharpened into analysis — I argued that [microlearning](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/06/28/an-opportunity-forecast-usable-microlearning/#comment-6268) delivers "a Timbit of microknowledge" rather than full understanding, praised the [immersive team's](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/07/05/week-8-immersive-experiences-in-education/#comment-6297) tiered path to implementation, and celebrated the [AI module's](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/07/26/week-11-artificial-intelligence-critical-thinking/#comment-6433) framing of whether the actual thinking still lands with the learner.

Co-developing the [Adaptive Technology & IEPs OER](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-20-adaptive-technology-ieps-oer/) taught me to design for student agency and data sovereignty, while reviewing ventures like [CISP Esports](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/08/12/cisp-esports-academy/#comment-6756) — where I valued capturing students' "competitive drive intrinsically" — and [Convia](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/08/09/convia-turning-the-classroom-into-a-shared-digital-experience/#comment-6770), whose strength lay in "shifting technology from an individual activity to a shared physical space," sharpened my EVA lens. Those threads converged in my own [venture pitch](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-08-09-curator-introduction/) for Curator and its Cadenza Studio MVP, where I stopped commenting and started building.

Across the cohort I kept threads collaborative rather than competitive, ending comments with open-ended prompts that handed the conversation back to the next learner rather than claiming the last word.

What I bring to the future of learning is the ability to "remix" the world around me and "jazz things up" — knowing how the game works and how the pieces fit together. That is the contribution I hope to keep making: carving out a corner of the market I want to inhabit rather than taking on the whole of it. Measured against the cohort's weblog activity, I place my contribution in the top 10%.
