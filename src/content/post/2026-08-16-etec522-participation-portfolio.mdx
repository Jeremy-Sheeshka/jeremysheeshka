## Reflective Statement (411 words)

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

I entered ETEC 522 as an Educator-Developer-Musician, intent on bridging two mindsets I rarely see in the same room: the compositional, improvisational energy of a teacher who designs curriculum, and the dispassionate, risk-aware discipline of a professional Educational Venture Analyst. My aim was to move beyond commenting on ideas and toward building and defending them. Those nine postings chart that arc.

Co-developing the [Adaptive Technology & IEPs OER](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-20-adaptive-technology-ieps-oer/) pushed me to design for student agency rather than compliance alone. The resource examined how adaptive tools could help public schools meet IEP legal obligations and forecast "Integrated Adaptive Learning Ecosystems" that hand agency back to learners. That instinct carried into my peer work: I argued that microlearning delivers "a Timbit of microknowledge rather than being the full holistic donut of understanding," and praised the critical-thinking team's "Calibrated Doubt" as a way to reward questioning rather than punish error. The throughline is constant — student agency, data sovereignty, and constructivist workflows that keep the thinking landed with the learner.

My analyst work ran in parallel. The [Founders on Parade](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-14-etec522-founders-parade/) profile of Adrian Holovaty taught me to look for inventor-entrepreneurs who solve lived problems, and my [Opportunity Audit](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-06-28-etec522-analyst-report/) of Sight Reading Factory forced a harder question: do useful tools always make sound investments? Those questions surfaced the structural risks I kept flagging for peers — hardware costs, market reach, and the privacy liabilities I explored in my own [venture pitch](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/posts/2026-08-09-curator-introduction/). Where analysis stopped, however, I built. Drawing on a background spanning carpentry, music, software development, and professional gaming, I turned Curator's thesis into the [Cadenza Studio](https://jeremysheeshka.ca/cadenza/) MVP — a working local-first application rather than a slide deck, letting me test my claims against reality.

Across the cohort I kept threads collaborative rather than competitive. Whether challenging [Freedrum's](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2025/07/18/freedrum-3-0-the-future-of-digital-drumming/#comment-6155) VR barrier ("I am buying the venture, not the dream"), weighing [Pool Maro's](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2024/01/28/pitch-pool-maro/#comment-6156) eleven-year diagnosis gap, or probing [CISP Esports'](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/2026/08/12/cisp-esports-academy/#comment-6756) hardware costs, I ended comments with open-ended prompts that invited the next learner to respond — elaborating on a peer's thinking and handing the conversation back rather than having the last word.

This dual position — OER creator, MVP builder, and rigorous peer reviewer — elevated the discourse of the course. By moving from critique to construction and back again, I aimed to model the very posture ETEC 522 teaches: ideas earn their place only when they are both well-argued and honestly stress-tested. Measured against the cohort's weblog activity, I place my contribution in the top 10%.

## Letter to the Next Cohort

Dear future ETEC 522 cohort — two small suggestions, offered with gratitude for a course that rewarded showing up:

- **Reduce platform friction.** The WordPress comment system silently rejected posts containing hyperlinks or emoji (a database error), costing participants hours and cluttering threads with test replies. A clearer error message, or a markdown-friendly comment field, would protect the very conversational flow this portfolio is meant to reward.
- **Pilot "VC Partner" Peer Circles.** Pair each learner with a small, stable circle of investors who review one another's pitches across the term, so critical feedback is sustained and structured rather than concentrated in the final weeks.
