For this IP I added a column for Ivan Illich to the paradigms chart in de Castell and Luke (1986). What I found was that he doesn't sit inside any one column, as he keeps the classical schoolmaster's drill for a learner who asks for it (Illich, 1971/1973a, p. 20) and the progressive trust in a learner's interests, while writing against the technocratic package.

<Ip3ParadigmChart />

## The tools, and what they replace

The tools Illich puts forward are ordinary ones like the tape recorder, since to him technology can serve "either independence and learning or bureaucracy and teaching" (pp. 79–80). What they were meant to replace is the school itself, which he counts as a tool as much as a drill or a power station (Illich, 1973b, ch. 2). The teaching machine is not on that list, as his worry was less the machine than who administers it (1973a, pp. 94, 104). Watters (2021) calls personalization "the pinnacle of a long-running dream of education technology" (p. 255), so I would wonder if what came after the technocratic column is closer to that worry than to his learning webs.

Several of Illich's cells could only be filled by writing what he would take away, which I suppose is the chart's constraint showing, since its rows are a school's own categories. It also has no row for tools, so I taped one along the bottom. Reading along it, what counts as knowing something changes with the tool, from reciting to an examiner, to a test score, to a packaged subskill, to whatever somebody can find and use without a certificate. I didn't add an Indigenous column, as de Castell and Luke end on the difference between an "imposed" literacy and a "derived" one (p. 107), and one written by me into a Western school's rows would be imposed again.
