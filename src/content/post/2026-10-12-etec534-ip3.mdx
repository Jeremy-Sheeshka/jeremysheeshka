For Intellectual Production #3 I added a column for Ivan Illich to the paradigms chart in de Castell and Luke (1986). Their three printed columns are retyped from pages 90 and 91, and the typed strips taped on are mine. Illich does not sit inside any one column, as he keeps the classical schoolmaster's drill for a learner who asks for it (Illich, 1971/1973a, p. 20) and the progressive trust in a learner's own interests, while writing mostly against the technocratic package.

<Ip3ParadigmChart />

## The tools, and what they were meant to replace

The tools Illich puts forward are ordinary ones, chosen because "technology is available to develop either independence and learning or bureaucracy and teaching" (p. 80): tape recorders where governments were buying TV stations, the telephone and the mail, a computer that only matches people asking about the same book, and a rented guitar with "taped guitar lessons and illustrated chord charts" (pp. 26, 79–80, 89). These were meant to replace the school itself, which he counts as a tool as much as a drill or a power station (Illich, 1973b, ch. 2), along with its graded curriculum and its certificates. The teaching machine is not on that list though, as he writes that tapes and programmed instruction "tend to reduce the need for recourse to human teachers of many skills" (1973a, p. 94).

His warning is that deschooling "could mean the advent of a 'brave new world' dominated by well-intentioned administrators of programmed instruction" (1973a, p. 104). Going by Watters's (2021) conclusion, where personalized learning software shapes behaviour "through nudges and rewards" (pp. 253–254), I would wonder if what came after the technocratic column is closer to that warning than to his learning webs.

Several of Illich's cells could only be filled in by writing what he would take away, and I suppose that is the constraint of the chart showing, since its rows are a school's own categories. It also has no row for tools, even though both readings are full of them, so I taped one along the bottom. I have not added an Indigenous column, as de Castell and Luke (1986) end on the difference between a literacy that is "imposed" and one that is "derived" (p. 107), and a column written by me into a Western school's rows would be another imposed one.
