## The tool and my context

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

This is my first year teaching Grade 4/5 in Prince Rupert, BC. One of my colleagues uses IXL primarily as a differentiated practice platform, so I chose to look into the Diagnostic Strand Analysis report in IXL Analytics (Canada edition) and the inferences it makes about students. While IXL's Real-Time Diagnostic is the assessment tool students use, the Diagnostic Strand Analysis is a teacher's report that uses those results to group students at similar levels within each of the four mathematics strands and recommends skills for small-group instruction ([IXL Learning, n.d.-a](#ref-ixl-nd-a), [n.d.-d](#ref-ixl-nd-d), [n.d.-e](#ref-ixl-nd-e)). 

I was not able to find any version number for this tool, so my evaluation is based on documentation from November 2026. As I have not used IXL before, I have relied on its documentation and research, in combination with independent studies, and my knowledge of BC law and school district policies. A trailing question I have is whether the suggested groups could help me see which grade-specific skills my students are ready for in a split class under the BC curriculum, and how far I could trust the report in my own planning.

</div>

## How I evaluated it

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

For my analysis I went with the Evaluation Framework for Learning Analytics (EFLA), whose questions cover the tool's data, the awareness and reflection it prompts, and its perceived impact on teaching ([Scheffel, 2018](#ref-scheffel-2018)). One limitation about the current EFLA is that it doesn't ask whether a tool's inferences are correct, and who has access to the data ([Scheffel et al., 2017](#ref-scheffel-2017)), so I added questions about validity and privacy. Finally, I drew on human-centred learning analytics (HCLA) to consider a teacher's agency, since a commercial product may offer limited scope for changing how the tool works ([Buckingham Shum et al., 2019](#ref-buckingham-shum-2019)). For each of these, I looked at what the report does, what IXL says, and what I could find outside of IXL.

</div>

## Applying the criteria

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

### 1. Data and validity: can I trust a level? (EFLA: Data; validity)

IXL recommends 45 to 60 questions to pin down a level and 5 to 10 each week ([IXL Learning, n.d.-b](#ref-ixl-nd-b)), though its help pages don't explain how a level is calculated. While IXL's own research calls its diagnostic valid, and in one Ontario school board, its overall levels matched the provincial math test on who was at or above grade level for 81% of grade 3, 6 and 9 students ([Schonberg, 2024](#ref-schonberg-2024), pp. 1, 3–4), I could find no study independent of IXL that has tested the tool's accuracy. IXL's validity studies in Ontario and Indiana only counted students with a pinpointed level (students who answered enough questions to get to a level), which in Indiana was 778 of 3,744 students in math ([IXL Learning, 2020](#ref-ixl-2020), p. 2; [Schonberg, 2024](#ref-schonberg-2024), p. 3). IXL's BC page for Grade 4 also lists no IXL skills under some of BC's curricular competencies, such as explaining and justifying mathematical ideas ([IXL Learning, n.d.-c](#ref-ixl-nd-c)). In my opinion, IXL’s overall levels seem plausible, but I am less confident about its suggested groupings for students who have not finished the Real-Time Diagnostic and how well those strand groupings align with BC’s curriculum, especially in a split class, where a Grade 4 and a Grade 5 student at the same strand level could end up in the same group while working toward different grade-level standards.

### 2. Awareness, reflection and agency (EFLA: Awareness and Reflection; HCLA)

The report would sort students in each strand into groups who, as IXL puts it, "would benefit from small group instruction" ([IXL Learning, n.d.-a](#ref-ixl-nd-a), para. 1). As I could not find independent research on the report, I turned to a study of primary teachers who use math dashboards from other adaptive programs daily. What I found was that teachers saw their knowledge of the student as essential for interpreting this kind of data, and valued being able to make independent choices within the program ([van Kessel et al., 2025](#ref-van-kessel-2025)). While I could choose to ignore recommended groups, I could not find any information in IXL's help pages about a teacher correcting a level.

### 3. Impact: would I teach more efficiently or effectively? (EFLA: Impact)

IXL says that its Diagnostic Snapshot (a benchmark mode of the same diagnostic) "improves student math achievement" ([Schonberg, 2023](#ref-schonberg-2023), p. 1), though the Snapshot cohorts used IXL about twice as much (p. 7). While a university study conducted under contract with IXL found a significant gain for IXL Math as a whole on one of two tests ([Copeland et al., 2023](#ref-copeland-2023)), an independent study of Grade 4 and 6 students found time spent on IXL was not significantly associated with math gains ([Hollands & Pan, 2018](#ref-hollands-pan-2018)). Keeping levels current would mean ongoing diagnostic questions for every student, which would be difficult to navigate alongside large class sizes and real-world technological limitations at my school.

### 4. Privacy: who has access?

IXL collects student names or other identifiers and answers, and may share these with teachers and school administrators, storing them in the United States or elsewhere, while saying it will not knowingly use student data to target advertising ([IXL Learning, 2026](#ref-ixl-2026)). BC's [Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act](#ref-fippa-1996) (1996, s. 69(5.3)) requires a public body to conduct a privacy impact assessment, which for newer systems must also cover sensitive personal information stored outside Canada ([B.C. Reg. 294/2021](#ref-bc-reg-294-2021)). SD52 has not yet posted its list of privacy assessments, and I was unable to find any list of tools available to its teachers, so I would first have to speak with HR about whether IXL analytic tools have been assessed ([School District 52, n.d.](#ref-sd52-nd)).
</div>

## What this adds up to

<div class="font-serif text-[1.0625rem] leading-[1.9]">

Most of the evidence was IXL's own studies about the Real-Time Diagnostic as a test or IXL as a whole, with nothing on the strand groups or on whether teachers who use these reports plan their lessons differently.

To me, having a report like this shows potential, but reads as if a few wrong answers from a student could mean missing out on math if the student's ability becomes misinterpreted by the analytics.

My recommendation to use IXL's Diagnostic Strand Analysis in my current context would be contingent upon 1) whether SD52 has assessed this tool, and 2) whether the Real-Time Diagnostic could be completed by students to fully inform the Strand Analysis (at which point, I would compare the tool's small groups to my own assessments and groupings). 
</div>
