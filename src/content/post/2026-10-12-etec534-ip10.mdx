For this IP I went back to my [IP#2](/posts/2026-09-26-etec534-ip2/) slides, where I looked at white-out through McLuhan's tetrad and Latour's actor-network theory. What I found was that both descriptions start with separate parties (a student, a mistake and a bottle) who are already there before anything happens. The bottle was either an extension of us or a stand-in that we handed the hiding to, so the agency was already inside someone from the start.

<Ip10Repaint />

Toohey (2018) describes new materialism as seeing "people, discourses, practices and things continually in relation, under construction and changing together" (p. 29). The "continually in relation" part is what my slides were missing, and Toohey explains it through Barad's idea of intra-action, where things are what they are through being entangled with one another (p. 29). A mistake in pencil just gets rubbed out, so I think it only becomes something to cover up when the ink won't come off, the page is supposed to be clean and somebody is going to mark it. This is to say that I would change my line that "what we delegated was the hiding", since the student, the mistake and the teacher it is hidden from all come out of that same situation.

The "under construction" part changes how I see the waiting, which in IP#2 I compared to having to "reflect on your mistake", as if the pause was there for the student. Charteris et al. (2017) quote Shaviro saying that we "must learn to work with" our tools (p. 812), and I think the drying is more like that, where the fluid has its own timing and the student goes along with it.

On my assembly slide I gave every party one seat and one line to say (even the ozone layer), which I suppose treats them all as if they were people. Bennett (2010) says that theories built on "active subjects and passive objects" are "thin descriptions", but also that not all participants are alike, since "persons, worms, leaves, bacteria, metals and hurricanes all have different types and degrees of power" (pp. 108–109). What I would add now is where the solvent goes, since the evaporation that dries the patch is also how it was breathed in and reached the ozone layer, and one of those solvents ended up phased out (King et al., 1985; Environment and Climate Change Canada, 2025). To me that is a small case of acknowledging "more nonhumans in more ways".

Hill (2017) borrows a question from Lenz Taguchi, asking "how does it work?" in place of "what does it mean?" (p. 4). For a patched page, I think that means asking what the pen, paper, fluid and my own marking are doing together before asking whose mistake is underneath. Hill also worried about children who "absolved themselves of any responsibility" (p. 13), and I would wonder the same about my students, which is partly why I painted over my IP#2 lines above and didn't delete them.
