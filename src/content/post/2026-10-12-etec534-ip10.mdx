For this IP#10 I went back to my [IP#2](/posts/2026-09-26-etec534-ip2/) slideshow, where I looked at white-out through McLuhan's tetrad and Latour's actor-network theory. While neither theory treated white-out as a neutral tool, I found that both started with human and non-human actors that were connected but already there before anything happened. For McLuhan and McLuhan (1988) the white-out was an extension of us, and for Latour (1992) it was a stand-in that took over the hiding, which in both cases puts the person first.

Toohey (2018) describes new materialism as seeing "people, discourses, practices and things continually in relation, under construction and changing together" (p. 29). My slides already had the white-out and the student acting on one another, so what "continually in relation" adds is that neither comes first. Toohey explains this through Barad's intra-action, where things are what they are through their entanglement. While a mistake in pencil can just get rubbed out, a mistake in ink has to be covered up, especially if the work is going to be marked and the student doesn't want the teacher to see it. This is to say that I would reconsider my line that "what we delegated was the hiding", since the student, the mistake and the teacher it is hidden from all simultaneously come out of that same situation.

In my IP#2 I treated the white-out as doing the hiding by itself, which leaves out what Charteris et al. (2017) describe as agency "produced in the in-between spaces of bodies and objects" (p. 811). They also write about "refusing a utilitarian conception of objects" (p. 811), and to me waiting for the patch to dry is the white-out's own timing that the student goes along with, rather than a moment to reflect on the mistake, which is what I compared it to there.

On my assembly slide I gave every party one seat and one line to say, which I suppose treats them all as if they were people. The Bennett quote that introduces this IP says theories of democracy built on "active subjects and passive objects" are "thin descriptions", but that participants are not all alike and have "different types and degrees of power". What I would add is that the white-out has a different kind of power than a seat and a line, since once the bottle dries up, the work it was covering for comes back to the student and the teacher, which to me is a small case of acknowledging "more nonhumans in more ways".

Hill (2017) describes diffractive reading as asking what something produces instead of what it means (p. 4). Hill also worried about children who blamed things and "absolved themselves of any responsibility" (p. 13), and so re-read two theories through each other, which is partly what I tried to do with my IP#2 and new materialism. For the page itself, I think that means asking what the pen, paper, white-out and reader are doing together before asking whose mistake is underneath.
