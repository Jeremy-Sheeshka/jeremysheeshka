Institutions sit on knowledge they can't safely hand to cloud AI: lesson recordings, student records, parent notes, billing information. In BC, privacy considerations make it a real liability to hand student or client data to a public cloud AI. No major AI product was built with that liability in mind, or with a way to integrate into the offline tech stacks these institutions already run.

<div class="clear-both"></div>


## Curator: The Local AI Infrastructure 

Curator is open-source, local-first AI infrastructure that runs on hardware a school or studio already owns, so that knowledge never has to leave the building to be useful.

Rather than sending data to the cloud for processing, Curator brings the AI to the data. It is designed to be deployed on the existing hardware a school or studio already has, keeping sensitive information like student records, lesson content, and billing data within the institution's physical control.

This is not a cloud product with a privacy policy that promises not to look at your data. It is infrastructure that makes it structurally impossible for your data to leave the building unless you explicitly choose to share it.

## Cadenza: The Flagship Application

Cadenza, our first application of this technology, shows what that looks like in practice: it listens to a lesson locally, drafts a summary, retrieves and transposes the right sheet music, answers voice questions against your own uploaded documents, and shares curated information with clients securely.

For example, you might ask it to find the Clarinet 3 part in a concert band arrangement and email it to two of your students. It's that easy. No digging through a filing cabinet. No drafting any emails. No trip to the photocopier!

Cadenza is our flagship application and technological wedge, which we are bringing to market, of Curator. It demonstrates that local-first AI is not a compromise or a stepping stone toward something cloud-based. It is the destination: a model where institutions own their data, own their infrastructure, and still get the full power of AI.

## The Pitch

I'm raising a $250,000 CAD pre-seed to fund continued development of Curator's offline-first, privacy-centred engine and the vertical B2B/B2B2C SaaS stacks built on it. The round buys one measurable milestone: a pilot cohort of 8–10 studios whose real usage data validates market interest and builds the evidence base for the next raise.

<div class="mt-4 space-y-4 text-sm text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed">
<p>Starting out on my journey through ETEC 522, I think I strongly identified as an intrapreneur, someone comfortable finding creative solutions and making small improvements within existing boundaries and capabilities. Taking on the role of an EVA in Assignment 1 forced me to check in to reality: to truly consider the context and complexities of how ideas get tested by markets and investors (market durability, future growth potential, the existing competition landscape, credibility) and what a venture pitch is actually made of. While I feel there is strength in the conceptual nature of my pitch overall, it remains quite broad and speculative at the overarching level, past the real-world frictions Cadenza Studio aims to smooth. There is weakness in several institutional unknowns, and despite the reduced barriers to entry through free distribution, the rate of growth may be unpredictable or non-existent altogether.</p>

<p>If I'm honest about where my inclinations land now, I feel they sit somewhere between the intrapreneur I started out as and the champion founder this assignment asked me to become. I lean toward this venture due to the fact that I believed it had served a purpose (privacy tools), had potential for growth in an area I was interested in and had experience in (music and technology), and aligned with my value of taking an instinctually human-first approach through proving one idea before moving from one step of the venture to another.</p>

<span class="text-lg">AI Reflections</span>

<div class="mt-4 space-y-4 text-sm text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 leading-relaxed">
<p>In developing and reflecting on my dual-pronged pitch for Curator and its flagship application, Cadenza, I have mixed thoughts to share. I began my journey brainstorming different concepts using tools like Claude and Qwen as sounding boards, only to find myself pulled in so many directions at once and suffering from scope creep. Through this, I became determined in my mindset and choice of venture, to the point that I had to create a minimum viable prototype of my own to try and implement these ideas in the future. What began as one idea grew to encompass two ideas with different market layers to consider: the present, what I could actually implement and realistically consider, and the direction the venture might take me in the future had it actually become investment-worthy. I had an entrepreneurial inclination to not stop until I found an ideal remix of existing tools I could effectively spin into its own thing with any meaningful potential in the real world.</p>

<p>I found myself chasing idealistic blue ocean ideas, trying to determine how my own background and knowledge could be implemented here and now, but ultimately found myself needing to feel comfortable representing the pitch I was trying to manifest. I chose this endeavour because I saw that no other competitors were offering anything similar to what I had in mind for a suite of vertical tech-stacks tailored towards offline solutions to administrative workflows. That said, I found that while the AI was effective at refining and aligning ideas to business mindsets, it lacked the lay of the land and real-world contextual edge to actually distill down and meaningfully apply what it was pitching.</p>

AI-generated content and suggestions were critically reviewed, revised, and approved by the author before publication.</p>
