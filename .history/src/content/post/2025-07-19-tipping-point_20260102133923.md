## Tipping Point

**A Project by Noralee Yarra, Jordan Swallow, and Jeremy Sheeshka**

<div class="dropdown-menu">
  <dl class="dropdown-group">
    <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">Introduction</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
        <p> One of our projects for ETEC 511: New Foundations of Educational Technology was to identify a significant shift and technological change in an educational technology where the adoption of a newer  technology displaces a previous one. <br> <br>
        For this group project, we have chosen to focus on the small-scale tipping point case of the technology adoption of Mathology through the lenses of: differentiated learning, differentiated instruction, and differentiated assessment.
        </p>
      </dd>
    </div>
    <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">What is Mathology? </dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
        <p>Mathology is a digital and print math resource that shifts learning from traditional paper-and-pencil tasks to more hands-on, engaging, and accessible learning experiences. It includes interactive activities, sample unit plans, “little books”, and built-in assessments that support all learners. Mathology supports differentiated learning by meeting students where they’re at in terms of current proficiency.
        </p>
                 <br>    
        <img src="/assets/images/blog/mathologylogo.png" alt="Mathology Logo" />
        <figcaption>etr.mathology.ca/ </figcaption>
      </dd>
      </div>
      <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">What systems or tools does it displace?</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
        <p>
Mathology represents a cross-curricular shift in the elementary math classroom by displacing traditional teaching tools and systems with digital and printed counterparts for educators. Whether it be dice, playing cards, cubes, blocks, worksheets, or even textbooks, Mathology represents a shift in physical artifacts of math teaching that focuses on an accessible learner- centered experience. <br><br>
        <img src="/assets/images/blog/tpticeberg.png" alt="Tipping Point Iceberg" />
        <figcaption> 
            <a href="/assets/images/blog/tpticeberg.png" target="_blank"> Click for Full Size </a>
         </figcaption> <br>
 By bundling together differentiated instructional materials, Mathology transcends analog resource management and displaces paper-and-pencil assessments through a single multi-modal platform.
        </p>
          </dd>
    </div>
       <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">Usability of Mathology</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
    <strong>Working with Woolgar</strong><br><br>
  One aspect of Mathology in particular worth noting is in how the tool takes into consideration the user’s experience, attention, and usability of the tool itself on a digital level.<br><br>
 The streamline process of which the content is distributed and accessed by students, along with user feedback cycles, are both factors which influence the platforms overall usability and actively shape how students interact with mathematics.
<br><br>
Mathology offers clickable manipulatives, and real-time information about the students learning on a dashboard. The instantaneous feedback, along with the prescribed sequences and actions of how students are able to interact with the on-screen material (dragging, clicking, uncovering etc.), are design choices that effectively make Mathology rise above the usability of traditional tools.

      <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">The Tipping Point of Mathematical Modelling</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
           <strong>Moving from In-Person to Online with Mathology</strong>
        <br><br>
        <p>
The COVID-19 pandemic marked a critical tipping point for digital educational resources. School systems across the globe shifted from in-person to online learning. The transition of mathematical modelling moved from concrete materials in the classroom to online platforms with digital resources. The pandemic created both a necessity and opportunity for its digital features that were accessible, user-friendly, and aligned with curriculum goals.
        </p>
      </dd>
    </div>

      <dt class="dropdown-question">Differentiated Education</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
        <p>
 A major benefit of Mathology that led it to a tipping point is the use of personalized algorithms to deliver a differentiated learning experience for each student. <br><br>
 <div style="text-align: center;">

 Exploring why traditional math instruction leaves students behind reinforces the case for Mathology’s tipping point, with a main reason being the lack of differentiation at a young age. Mathology addresses this through its focus on differentiated learning, understanding that each student has strengths and weaknesses.
 <br><br>
   <div style="text-align: center;">
 <img src="/assets/images/blog/numbers.png" alt="Numbers" />
 </div><br>
 Differentiated instruction allows the teacher to play a role in creating more personalized lessons and assignments that challenge students with content at their current ability level using data analytics. Mathology acts as a critical stepping stone to learning math in an engaging and non-threatening way.
  <br><br>
        </p>
        </dd>

      <dt class="dropdown-question">Media Convergence</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
        <p>
   While Mathology addresses many core challenges, there are challenges as well. Kate Crawford identified several major issues when large international tech corporations operate with little regulatory oversight. (2021, p. 149).
       <br> <br><div style="text-align: center;">
 <img src="/assets/images/blog/monopoly.png" alt="Monopoly" />
 <div><br>
 Mathology is owned by Pearson Education, which already holds a level of public wariness due to its size and for-profit structure. As Crawford warns, a concentration of economic power in a few tech companies can lead to a Global North–centered system that also incentivizes profit over pedagogy. 
 <br><br><div style="text-align: center;">
 <img src="/assets/images/blog/SOS.png" alt="SOS" />
 <div>
  <br>
        </p>
        </dd>
    </div>
    <div class="dropdown-item">
      <dt class="dropdown-question">Digital Labour & Sustainability</dt>
      <dd class="dropdown-answer">
       With the tipping point case of Mathology, the issues of digital labour and sustainability are intertwined. On one hand, teachers have a more efficient and pedagogically effective system. On the other hand, large tech organizations are able to externalize labour costs through outsourcing. (Crawford, 2021, p. 63).
        </p><br>
         <div style="text-align: center;">
 <img src="/assets/images/blog/help.png" alt="Help" />
 <div><br>
        <p>
         However, these tools also risk creating new forms of invisible labour in the form of constant data monitoring. An example Kate Crawford uses is the prominence of young male workaholics who are the ones primarily building these tools for audiences with completely different needs. (Crawford, 2021, p. 77).
        </p><br>
