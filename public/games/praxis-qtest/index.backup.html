Curriculum Otherwise · a reimagined sight-reading studio

Rhythm literacy built with the room, not around it.

Praxis began as a standard rhythm drill: a fixed canon, a metronome, a red mark for a wrong answer. This studio reimagines that premise as a universal, constructivist environment — one that assumes every student already arrives with rhythm, and asks the platform to notice, hold, and build from what they bring in.

What changed, and why

Three design moves reshape the original tool. Look for the small RESIST, RELATE, and IMAGINE tags throughout the studio — open any of them, or use the tabs on the right edge of the screen, to read the annotation attached to that decision.

How to move through it

Start in the Dashboard to choose a rhythm collection. From there, practice alone in Free Play, or negotiate a shared phrase with a partner in Trade Fours. When you're ready, visit the Gallery for the literature behind this redesign and one prompt for trying it yourself.

Gallery · literature and an invitation

Read what shaped this, then try it yourself.

This studio was built in conversation with a handful of ideas about datasets, feedback, and who a "universal" exercise is designed for. The reading below informed specific decisions inside the app; the prompt at the end is for you.

Literature consulted
  • Onuoha, M. (2016). The Library of Missing Datasets. Treats absence itself as a design choice — whose rhythms, whose music, never made it into the training set. Sits directly behind the Cultural Datasets feature and its RESIST annotation.
  • Paris, D., & Alim, H. S. (2014). Culturally sustaining pedagogy. Argues classrooms should sustain, not just accommodate, the cultural practices students already bring. Behind the decision to let a family or community collection sit beside "Afro-Cuban Claves" without ranking one tradition above another.
  • Moll, L., Amanti, C., Neff, D., & Gonzalez, N. (1992). Funds of knowledge. Frames a household's everyday practices as intellectual resources, not a gap to close. Behind the Local Family Database preset.
  • Freire, P. (1970). Pedagogy of the Oppressed. Critiques the "banking model," where a teacher deposits correct answers into a passive student. Behind moving feedback away from a pass/fail score.
  • CAST (2018). Universal Design for Learning Guidelines. Argues that flexible means of representation, action, and engagement serve every learner. Behind offering listening, clapping, and chanting as equally valid routes through a rhythm.
  • Varela, F., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). The Embodied Mind. Source of "enactive" cognition — understanding built through embodied action, not just correction. Behind the Enactive Scaffold.

Swap this list for your group's own Part A sources — it's built to hold short, paraphrased entries like these.

A doing prompt for the cohort

Open the Cultural Datasets card and select Local Family Database. Then go to Trade Fours and play through a phrase with a partner as if it came from your own household — a lullaby, a chant, a rhythm from a game, anything with a pulse.
